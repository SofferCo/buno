Images 1, 2 and 3 are example sheets from one logo designer. In every row: left, a photo; right, the logo mark he designed from it. Image 4 is a photo of a new animal. Design the mark he would design for image 4.

He does not trace the photo. He rebuilds the animal from a handful of big simple pieces, exactly as the examples show:
- The body is one heavy block with long straight edges and one or two large clean arcs.
- Legs, arms, beaks, horns, wings and tails are simple pieces: a straight tapered wedge, a bar bent once at the joint, or a single curved stroke of even thickness. Thin parts are drawn heavier than in life. Feet end in a flat straight cut.
- Detail is thrown away. Fur, feathers, scales, spots, fingers, toes, body segments and wing ribs are not drawn. A row of repeated things (spines, feathers, tines, claws) becomes two or three bold ones, or none.
- Where one part lies in front of another, a thin white gap of even width separates them. A mark has at most three such gaps.
- Every edge is either dead straight or part of a true circle. Corners are crisp. No wobble, no small bumps, no scallops, no soft blobs.
- The whole outline could be drawn with about twenty strokes of a ruler and a compass.

Keep from the photo: the pose, the direction the animal faces, the real proportions of head, body and limbs, and the true number of legs that can be seen. An animal seen from the front is perfectly mirror-symmetric. The eye is one small white dot where the real eye is (an almond for a cat-like eye).

Output: one solid black mark on a pure white square background, filling the square. Pure black and pure white only, crisp edges, no grey, no outline strokes, no guide lines, no text, no photo.
