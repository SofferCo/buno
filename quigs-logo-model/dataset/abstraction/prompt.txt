Images 1, 2 and 3 are example sheets from one logo designer. In every row: left, a photo; right, the black logo mark he drew from that photo. Study how minimal his marks are: one bold solid silhouette, nothing that is not needed to recognise the animal, and a very few white gaps that let one part read in front of another. Image 4 is a new animal. Draw its logo mark the same way. MINIMAL: one solid black silhouette, as simple as it can be while still unmistakably this animal. No texture and no decoration: no wing ribs, no body segments, no stripes, no scales, no fur, no rows of small lines. NEGATIVE SPACE (required, it is what makes the mark read): wherever one part of the animal lies in front of another, cut one white gap of even width along the edge of the front part, so the two read as separate shapes. Always do this for: the body in front of spread wings (the gap runs down both sides of the belly); a folded wing lying on a bird's body (the gap outlines the wing, and the wing's trailing edge ends in three or four broad feather tips); a rear or far-side leg where it passes behind the body or behind another leg; an arm in front of the chest. If the species has one signature marking it is recognised by (for example the large round plate on an iguana's cheek), cut it as one clean white shape at its real place. A segmented tail, body or claw stays one solid shape: never cut gaps between its segments. Between one and four white gaps in all, besides the eye: each a bold, clean, simple shape, never a thin scratchy line, never a pattern. CONSTRUCTION: the outline is made of arcs of perfect circles of a few sizes, none larger than a quarter of the animal, meeting at clean points, plus a few straight lines where a part is truly straight. No ellipses, no free curves, no wobble. FIDELITY: exact pose; every part at its real size and place; the head no larger than the real head; exactly the limbs visible in the photo, never one more or one less; thin limbs drawn as clear simple shapes, evenly spaced. Repeated parts (spines, fingers, legs) are few, equal and evenly spaced. An animal facing the viewer is perfectly mirror-symmetric. The eye is a small white circle exactly where the real eye is, or an almond for a cat-like eye. Output: one solid black mark on a pure white square background, filling the square. Pure black and pure white only, crisp edges, no grey, no outlines, no guide lines, no text, no photo.
