Images 1, 2 and 3 are example sheets from one logo designer. In every row: left, a photo; right, the black logo mark he drew from that photo. Study exactly how he abstracts an animal: a few long perfectly straight edges; a handful of arcs of perfect circles, none larger than about a quarter of the animal; flat planes instead of soft curves; the tail one bold simple shape; limbs as simple straight-sided shapes, the far limbs detached from the body or separated from it by a thin white gap; no fur, no scales, no small bumps, no wobble. The outline is made only of straight lines and arcs of circles: no ellipses, no free curves. Image 4 is a new animal, cut out of its photo. Draw the logo mark of this exact animal the way this designer would. Keep its exact pose and keep every part the same size and in the same place as in image 4: the head no larger than the real head, the same head, body, tail and limb positions. Every limb, paw and hand that is visible in the photo is drawn as its own clear simple shape. Redraw the outline with his bold geometric simplification instead of tracing it. The eye is one small white circle placed exactly where the animal's eye is. Output: one solid black mark on a pure white square background. Pure black and pure white only, crisp edges, no grey, no outlines, no guide lines, no text, no photo.
