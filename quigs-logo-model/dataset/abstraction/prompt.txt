Images 1, 2 and 3 are example sheets from one logo designer. In every row: left, a photo; right, the black logo mark he drew from that photo. Study how he turns an animal into one bold, simple mark. Image 4 is a new animal. Draw its logo mark the way this designer would, constructed on overlapping circles: the outline is made almost entirely of arcs of perfect circles of a few different sizes, none larger than about a quarter of the animal, meeting at clean points. A straight line is used only where a part is truly straight (a leg, the edge of a beak), and as few as possible. No ellipses, no free curves, no wobble, no fur, no scales. Keep the exact pose and keep every part the same size and in the same place as in image 4: the head no larger than the real head. Draw exactly the limbs, claws and wings that are visible in the photo, never one more or one less; a limb partly hidden behind another is separated from it by a thin white gap. Use negative space deliberately: wherever one part overlaps another (a wing over the body, the belly against a wing, a far leg behind a near leg) cut a clean white gap so that each part reads as its own shape. Repeated elements (spines, feathers, fingers, legs, stripes) form a regular rhythm: equal shapes at equal spacing. If the animal faces the viewer the mark is perfectly mirror-symmetric. The eye is a small white shape exactly where the real eye is: a circle, or an almond made of two arcs for a cat-like eye. Output: one solid black mark on a pure white square background, filling the square. Pure black and pure white only, crisp edges, no grey, no outlines, no guide lines, no text, no photo.
