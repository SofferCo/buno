Images 1, 2 and 3 are example sheets from one logo designer. In every row: left, a photo; right, the black logo mark he drew from that photo. Study how minimal his marks are: one bold solid silhouette, nothing that is not needed to recognise the animal. Image 4 is a new animal. Draw its logo mark the same way. MINIMAL: one solid black silhouette, as simple as it can be while still unmistakably this animal. No interior lines at all: no feather lines, no wing ribs, no body segments, no stripes, no scales, no fur, no texture. White is used only where it is essential: the eye, and at most three clean gaps that separate a part from the part behind it (a far leg from a near leg, a wing or an arm from the body). CONSTRUCTION: the outline is made of arcs of perfect circles of a few sizes, none larger than a quarter of the animal, meeting at clean points, plus a few straight lines where a part is truly straight. No ellipses, no free curves, no wobble. FIDELITY: exact pose; every part at its real size and place; the head no larger than the real head; exactly the limbs visible in the photo, never one more or one less; thin limbs drawn as clear simple shapes, evenly spaced. Repeated parts (spines, fingers, legs) are few, equal and evenly spaced. An animal facing the viewer is perfectly mirror-symmetric. The eye is a small white circle exactly where the real eye is, or an almond for a cat-like eye. Output: one solid black mark on a pure white square background, filling the square. Pure black and pure white only, crisp edges, no grey, no outlines, no guide lines, no text, no photo.
